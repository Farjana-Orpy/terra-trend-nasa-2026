03 / UNDERSTANDING THE DATA

Understand every clue.

Learn how the data are prepared, analyzed, and interpreted.

01 · DATA SOURCE

NASA POWER

TerraTrend retrieves historical monthly Earth-system estimates from NASA POWER for the coordinates and period selected.

These are gridded data products, not measurements from a weather station at the exact selected point.

02 · PICK THE PLACE

Where on Earth?

Choose a city from the list or click the world map. Latitude and longitude are simply the north/south and east/west numbers that identify that spot.

03 · PICK WHAT TO MEASURE

Which Earth indicator?

Temperature = how warm the air is. Precipitation = how much rain falls. Humidity = moisture in air. Wind speed = how fast air moves. Solar energy = sunlight energy reaching the surface.

04 · PICK THE YEARS

When did change occur?

Select the first and last calendar years, then click Analyze NASA data. Only complete years with all 12 monthly records are included.

05 · READ THE GRAPH

How did it change?

The chart line connects values for successive years. The dashed trend line estimates the overall long-term direction. Hover over or focus a point to see its year and value. A zigzag does not always mean a long-term trend.

06 · READ THE FINDINGS

What do the numbers say?

Trend per decade = estimated change in ten years. Estimated change = change along the fitted line across the period. Significance tests evidence that the linear slope differs from zero. Years analyzed counts usable complete years.

07 · COMPARE TWO PLACES

Could trends differ?

Under Compare, choose two cities and click Compare evidence. You will see two series for the same years and indicator. Their slopes may differ, but the tool does not statistically test the difference between the slopes.

08 · SAVE OR SHARE

Keep your evidence

CSV downloads annual values for further analysis; Copy findings copies the interpretation; Print / save PDF creates a printable report with the source and methods.

HOW TO INTERPRET A RESULT

Read the direction and rate

A positive slope indicates an estimated increase; a negative slope indicates an estimated decrease. The rate per decade describes the fitted change over ten years.

What does “p-value” mean?

A p-value below 0.05 is commonly called statistically significant under the model assumptions. It is not proof of what caused the change and does not guarantee the trend will continue. The confidence interval shows the uncertainty in the estimated slope.

04 / THE SCIENCE BEHIND THE SIGNAL

Beautiful charts.
Defensible conclusions.

Nothing hidden. Understand exactly how each Terra Trend conclusion is made.

01 — SOURCE
◉

Ask the Earth

We query NASA POWER's monthly point API for historical Earth-system estimates at the selected coordinates. Each chart traces back to its source request.

02 — PREPARE
▥

Respect the units

We calculate day-weighted annual means for temperature and other rates, and annual totals for precipitation. Years missing a month are excluded.

03 — TEST
∿

Find the trend

Ordinary least squares estimates the annual slope. A two-sided t-test, p-value, and 95% confidence interval describe statistical evidence.

04 — INTERPRET
✦

Tell it responsibly

We report effect size, uncertainty, and caveats. Statistical significance is not causation, and a non-significant result does not prove stability.

WHY THIS MATTERS

One planet. More than one interpretation.

NASA POWER is a gridded Earth science data product, not a thermometer physically installed at your exact selected point. Meteorological data combine measurements and models. Regional differences, missing months, and year-to-year dependence affect how conclusions should be read.
